# Supply (Economics)

## What is Supply?

In economics, **supply** refers to the total amount of a specific good or service that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various possible prices during a given time period, holding all other factors constant. The relationship between price and quantity supplied is formally described by the **Law of Supply**, which posits that—ceteris paribus (all else being equal)—an increase in a good's price leads to an increase in the quantity supplied, as higher prices incentivize producers to increase output to capture greater potential revenue and profit.

# Examples & Usage

| Concept / Mechanism | Description | 
| ----- | ----- | 
| **Law of Supply** | States that, other things equal, an increase in a good's price leads to an increase in quantity supplied | 
| **Supply Curve** | A graphical representation showing the upward-sloping relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied | 
| **Determinants of Supply** | Non-price factors that shift the entire supply curve, including production technology, input costs, taxes, and producer expectations | 
| **Market Supply** | The horizontal sum of all individual producer supplies for a specific good or service in a given market | 
| **Elasticity of Supply** | A measure of how sensitive the quantity supplied of a good is to a change in its price |
