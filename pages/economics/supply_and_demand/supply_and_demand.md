# Supply and Demand

## What is Supply and Demand?

Supply and Demand is an economic model designed to determine price and quantity in a market. The model posits that, holding other factors constant, in a competitive market, the unit price for a particular good, or other traded item such as labor or liquid financial assets, will vary until it settles at a point where the quantity demanded (at current price) equals the quantity supplied (at current price), resulting in an economic equilibrium for price and quantity.

# Examples & Usage

| Concept / Mechanism | Description | 
| ----- | ----- | 
| **Market Equilibrium** | The exact price point where the supply curve and demand curve intersect, meaning quantity demanded equals quantity supplied | 
| **Law of Demand** | States that, other things equal, an increase in a good's price leads to a decrease in quantity demanded | 
| **Law of Supply** | States that, other things equal, an increase in a good's price leads to an increase in quantity supplied | 
| **Price Ceiling** | A government-imposed limit on how high a price can be charged for a product (often causes shortages) | 
| **Price Floor** | A government-imposed minimum price below which a good or service cannot be sold (often causes surpluses) |
