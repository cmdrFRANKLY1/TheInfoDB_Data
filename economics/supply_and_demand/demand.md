# Demand (Economics)

## What is Demand?

In economics, **demand** refers to a consumer's desire, willingness, and ability to purchase a specific good or service at various possible prices during a given time period, holding all other factors constant. The relationship between price and quantity demanded is formally described by the **Law of Demand**, which posits that—ceteris paribus (all else being equal)—an increase in a good's price leads to a decrease in the quantity demanded, as higher costs discourage consumers or price them out of the market. Conversely, a decrease in price increases the quantity demanded due to higher consumer affordability and perceived value.

# Examples & Usage

| Concept / Mechanism | Description | 
| ----- | ----- | 
| **Law of Demand** | States that, other things equal, an increase in a good's price leads to a decrease in quantity demanded | 
| **Demand Curve** | A graphical representation showing the downward-sloping relationship between the price of a good and the quantity demanded | 
| **Determinants of Demand** | Non-price factors that shift the entire demand curve, including consumer income, preferences, prices of related goods (substitutes and complements), and population size | 
| **Market Demand** | The horizontal sum of all individual consumer demands for a specific good or service in a given market | 
| **Elasticity of Demand** | A measure of how sensitive the quantity demanded of a good is to a change in its price |
